When learners observe first and explain later, the discussion has more evidence and fewer memorized answers. This filler post gives the CMS a medium-complexity example for a learning routine.

The activity begins with a simple demonstration and a quiet observation round. Students write what they notice without using vocabulary from the chapter, then compare lists before the facilitator introduces formal terms.

That small delay creates better questions. Learners can point to specific details, disagreements, and surprising changes instead of trying to guess the answer the adult expects.
