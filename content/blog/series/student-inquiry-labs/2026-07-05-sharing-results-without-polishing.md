Not every investigation ends neatly. This post models how students can share uncertainty without treating it as failure, which is a useful message for early lab work.

Groups present three things: what they tried, what their evidence suggests, and what they would change if they had one more round. The language keeps the discussion honest while still valuing the work.

For facilitators, this is also a diagnostic moment. Unpolished explanations reveal where students are connecting ideas and where the next session needs a clearer bridge.
