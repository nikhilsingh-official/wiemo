Data tables help students coordinate, but the table should support thinking instead of becoming the whole activity. This placeholder post describes a practical compromise.

Each group records the variable they changed, the value they measured, two repeated trials, and one source of uncertainty. That is enough structure to compare results while leaving room for real decisions.

The final discussion asks which rows can be compared fairly. It also asks which rows should be kept separate because the groups changed too many things at once.
