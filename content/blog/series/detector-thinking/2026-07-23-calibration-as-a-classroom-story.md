Calibration can be taught as a story about agreement between instruments, known references, and measurements the team does not yet trust. That story is more interesting than a hidden technical chore.

The classroom version gives groups three measuring tools that disagree slightly. Students measure the same object, compare results, choose a reference, and decide how much correction is reasonable.

From there, the post can connect to detector systems where the stakes are higher but the logic is familiar: before interpreting a rare signal, the experiment has to understand its ordinary measurements.
