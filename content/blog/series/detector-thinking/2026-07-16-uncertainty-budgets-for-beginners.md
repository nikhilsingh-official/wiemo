Students can handle uncertainty when it is broken into readable sources: instrument limits, timing choices, repeated trials, and model assumptions. This post gives the hardest series a math-adjacent entry without requiring advanced equations.

The lesson asks groups to list every place a measurement could drift. They sort those sources into random variation, systematic offset, human timing, and limits of the model they are using.

The useful shift is conceptual. Uncertainty stops being a confession that the answer is bad and becomes a map of what the team understands about the measurement.
