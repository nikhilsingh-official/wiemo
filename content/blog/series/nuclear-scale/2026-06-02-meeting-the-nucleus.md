The nucleus is far too small to inspect with an ordinary microscope. Instead, scientists build a picture of it by sending particles toward matter and studying what changes direction.

Students can model the idea with a hidden object, a set of rolling marbles, and a record of where each marble emerges. The pattern does not reveal every detail, but it rules out some possible shapes and supports others.

The activity introduces a useful scientific habit: a model is not a photograph. It is an explanation constrained by the evidence we can collect.
