Radioactive decay can look like a collection of symbols to memorize. A more durable route begins by asking what changed and what remained balanced on both sides of the equation.

![accelerator-ring](/images/accelerator-ring.svg "Accelerator"){height="200" width="200"}

Students use number cards for proton count and nucleon count. For each decay, they account for every value before attaching the labels alpha, beta, or gamma.

Conservation becomes the checking tool. If the totals do not balance, the proposed decay story is incomplete.
