No one can predict exactly when a particular unstable nucleus will decay. A large collection, however, follows a pattern that can be measured and compared.

Groups toss a set of coins, remove one outcome after each round, and graph how many remain. Individual groups look noisy, while the combined class data approaches a smoother curve.

The model is imperfect, and discussing its limits matters. Coins wait for a toss; nuclei do not. What the model captures is the relationship between chance at the individual scale and regularity in a population.
