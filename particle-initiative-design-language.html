01

Direction

The site should feel like an instrument reading out the physical world — precise, dark, and quiet, with a single field of ATLAS-blue points doing the talking. Restraint is the aesthetic. The particles are never decoration; they are the argument.

Principle 01

Black is the vacuum

True black (#000000) in the hero, a hair of blue-black elsewhere. Empty space is not wasted space — it is the void the particles live in. Never fill it to feel "complete."

Principle 02

One hue, many energies

The palette is essentially monochromatic: ATLAS blue, ranging from deep azure to hot cyan-white cores. Colour variation encodes energy, not category. Reserve warmth for a single call to action.

Principle 03

Instrument readouts

Small, letter-spaced monospace labels sit in the corners like a detector's telemetry — 24K POINTS · GPU-READY. This readout motif is the site's signature, repeated on every screen.

Principle 04

Motion means physics

Nothing animates for flourish. Particles drift with thermal jitter, tracks curve in a magnetic field, events resolve over ~1s. If a motion can't be justified physically, cut it.

02

Contrast — Ink on Black clears WCAG AAA for body text. ATLAS blue #0B80C3 on black passes AA for large text and UI, but not AA for small body copy: use Beam #33B4EC or lighter for any blue text under 18px. Amber #F2B441 on black is high-contrast — that visibility is exactly why it's rationed. All blues remain distinguishable under the common deuteranopia / protanopia CVD types since the system separates by lightness, not hue.

04

Particle & motion language

One rendering system, used everywhere: the masthead field, the hero, section transitions. Get these rules right and every particle on the site looks like it came from the same detector.

Colour spread

Each point samples the blue ramp weighted toward ATLAS blue: roughly 70% ATLAS, 22% Beam, 6% Deep, 2% Core. The rare hot cores are what make the field read as luminous rather than flat.

#0B80C3 ×70#33B4EC ×22#0A4E74 ×6#BDE8FB ×2
Size & glow

1–2px points at 1× DPR; cores get a soft additive glow (2–3px blur). Use globalCompositeOperation = "lighter" so overlapping points brighten — that additive bloom is the reference's whole look.

Thermal jitter

Even at rest, every point drifts on a low-amplitude sine (±0.5px, individual phase). The field is never perfectly still — matter has temperature.

Curvature

Where points form tracks (the collision hero), they follow curved arcs, not straight lines — charged particles bend in a magnetic field. Opposite "charges" curve opposite ways. This is the single detail that separates the look from a generic node graph.

Density budget

Cap ≈ 24k points on desktop (matching the reference), scale down on mobile by viewport area. Target 60fps; drop count before dropping framerate.

Reduced motion

Honour prefers-reduced-motion: render one resolved static frame, no drift, no re-collide loop. Also pause the loop on visibilitychange when the tab is hidden.

05

The hero — collision is the method

Two beams meet at a central vertex; the collision sprays curved tracks outward, and each track is a way into the site. It resolves over ~1s on load, and re-fires on a "re-collide" trigger so it never reads as a fixed diagram. Rendered in the language above: ATLAS-blue tracks, hot vertex, detector rings, corner readouts.

Alternate

Morph field. Where the collision is overkill — an interior page header, a loading state — fall back to the reference's waving point field morphing between everyday objects (cup → bike → phone) with a rotating caption. Same rendering system, lower intensity. Keep the collision as the front-door method and the morph as connective tissue.

06

Layout & components

Wide margins, a single column of content, and the readout motif anchoring the corners. Structure stays quiet so the particle moments carry the weight.

Grid

Max content width ~1080px, centred, with a fluid gutter (clamp(20px, 5vw, 64px)). Long-form text capped at ~64 characters. One column — resist multi-column density; the vacuum is the point.

Spacing scale

A 4px base step: 4 · 8 · 12 · 16 · 24 · 40 · 64 · 96. Section padding scales fluidly from 56px (mobile) to 120px (desktop).

Readout corners

Every full-screen view carries four monospace corner labels: identity (TL), physics datum (TR), telemetry (BL/BR). This is the signature — treat it as fixed furniture, not decoration to omit when busy.

Navigation

On the hero, nav lives on the tracks. On inner pages, collapse to a slim top bar: mono small-caps links, a hairline #151C28 underline, Beam on hover.

Cards = channels

Content blocks are "channels" (as in decay channels): panel #080B12, 1px #151C28 border, 12–14px radius, a mono eyebrow, a Space Grotesk title. Hover lifts the border to Deep azure.

Borders & radius

Hairline borders only (1px). Radius 8–16px — soft enough to feel modern, never pill-round except on the mono nav tokens where it echoes particle tracks.

07

Voice & copy

The audience is students deciding whether this is for them. The tone is a working physicist who's genuinely glad you asked — precise, never gatekeeping. Curiosity over credentials.

Do

Plain, then precise

Lead with the everyday ("everything around you is particles"), then earn the detail. Let one real datum — √s = 13 TeV — do more than a paragraph of adjectives.

Do

Invite, don't impress

Empty states and CTAs are invitations: "Come collide with us," not "Submit application." Actions keep one name through the whole flow.

Don't

No jargon walls

Never open with terminology a first-year hasn't met. A term can appear once it's been made welcome — labels teach, they don't test.

Don't

No hype physics

Skip "unlock the secrets of the universe." The subject is astonishing on its own; overselling it reads as insecurity. Specific beats cosmic.
